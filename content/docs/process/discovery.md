## TL;DR

Discovery is the loop that keeps the team honest about the problem. It runs from day one of the engagement until the last day — and it answers the same question on repeat: *what problem are we actually solving, and for whom*?

## What it is

Discovery is the smallest set of activities that turns a fuzzy ask into a clear problem worth solving. It runs in two halves: **diverge** to surface what is really going on, then **converge** to a single working hypothesis that the next loop can test.

In practice it is rarely a kickoff workshop. It is a recurring half-day each week, with the rest of the week spent talking to users, reading code, and writing down what we saw.

## What happens

- Short user conversations — 5 to 8 a week, written up the same day
- Reading the existing code, support tickets, and analytics to ground claims
- Naming one working hypothesis per week and a way to falsify it
- Surfacing risks the team might otherwise meet for the first time in delivery

## What you get

- A weekly note listing what was learned, what changed, and what is still open
- One working hypothesis in plain English, attached to a falsifiable check
- A short list of questions the team should answer before any design or build work starts

## Cadence

- First two weeks: heavier — typically two half-days per week
- Steady state: one half-day per week, on the same day, all engagement long
- Skipped only if a milestone is actively shipping and you have asked for a focused week

## What I need from you

- Access to at least one weekly user conversation (recorded, with consent)
- The last quarter of support tickets, churn notes, or sales call transcripts — anything that records real customer words
- A short written brief in your own words, even if it is rough — rough briefs make better discoveries than polished ones

## What comes next

The working hypothesis feeds [Design](/docs/process/design/). Discovery does not stop when design starts — it continues in the background and the two loops read each other's notes.
